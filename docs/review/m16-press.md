# M16 press-law review: statements about named bodies

Spec §4.1 item 3 and §4.9 item 8; legal frame in `docs/review/m16-legal.md` §3 (§ 19 Abs. 1 MStV: news are to be checked for content, origin and truth with the care the circumstances require). This is the table for the user's review (Task 10). No content or config file was changed; every rewording below is a proposal, and none ships before the user has reviewed it (ruling P2: the last column, `Status`, starts `open`).

## Method

`uv run python tools/press_review.py --out FILE` lists every reader-facing sentence of both editions that names a person, party, administration or organisation, uses an actor-implying verb ("rolled back", "zurückgebaut", "stop" …), or states a cause or a judgement in a paragraph that names an actor. It reads the English and German content files, the UI catalogues (`content/ui/*.yaml`), the flagged-year explanations (`docs/review/m3_timeseries{,.de}.md`), `config/limitations.yaml` and the config prose that `velometer.config.reader_prose` declares reader-facing (differences, rankings, plan and report notes, counter notes, the figures.csv notes, NUTS3 notes). Names come from data: the bodies of `config/organisations.yaml` with their names and kinds in `content/organisations/`, and every source publisher in `content/sources/`; offices, parties and coalition colours are lists in the tool. On 2026-10-21 it listed **480 candidate sentences** (245 English, 235 German, from 78 files; by reason: names-person 4, names-party 2, names-administration 205, names-organisation 284, causal-claim 28, judgement 25). Every candidate was read. **404 were cut as false positives** (breakdown below): mostly the method comparisons that restate what Clean Cities, QECIO, BikeNetKit or a city publish, and names used as labels. A read of the paragraphs around each named body added 21 sentences the tool had missed (marked † in the Key column: absence claims, "inference" sentences and German twins whose wording escaped the lists). That leaves **48 claims in 96 rows** (English and German twins on adjacent rows). Not read as candidates: the excerpts in `content/sources/` (verbatim, attributed quotations and their glosses, checked under § 51 UrhG in the legal review) and the app templates, which hold no reader prose outside the UI catalogues.

Judged as a careful German media lawyer would, plainly: a statement of fact (Tatsachenbehauptung) must be true and provable from the cited source; an opinion (Meinungsäußerung) is free if it is marked as one and rests on stated facts; a report of a suspicion or of another outlet's verdict must say whose it is; identifiable individuals need the most care. Nothing here is a high risk: no sentence names a private person or a party member, none alleges wrongdoing, and every claim has a cited source. The medium rows are places where a press verdict or a single press lead is printed as our own fact, a ranking figure called irreproducible, and three published notes that say of themselves that they still need checking.

## ADR 0006: mapped km, not built km

Every reader-facing sentence with "built", "build", "construction", "gebaut", "Bau" or "new cycle paths" was read (76 sentences). OSM lengths are always called mapped km; "built" appears only in the mapped-not-built notes, in explanations that deny a build ("not a loss of built infrastructure", "no official build figure is cited"), for OSM's `construction` tag, and for official figures and plans, attributed to their publisher. One wording slips: "everything **we map**" in the Clean Cities rows for Paris (claims 8–10 above). We read the map; mappers map. The proposed rewording says "everything mapped there", which the German ("alles, was dort … kartiert ist") already does. Borderline, kept: "new cycling infrastructure built by the city in 2025, all types" (`config/plans/reports_notes_de.yaml`) describes an official reported row, not our data; "as reported by the city" would make that explicit.
