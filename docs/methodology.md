# Methodology

velometer measures protected cycling infrastructure in European cities from OpenStreetMap (OSM). It does so for yearly snapshots and for one current snapshot. It sets the results against published rankings and official figures. Our own pipeline is the primary source for every number. External figures are comparison layers and are never blended into our numbers [ADR 0001].

All OSM lengths are **mapped km**, not built km. They show what is mapped in OSM, not what was built [ADR 0006].

## What is measured

The unit of classification is the **way side**, not the way. Every road is split into a left and a right side, using the tags `cycleway`, `cycleway:both`, `cycleway:left` and `cycleway:right`, plus the legacy `cycleway=opposite*` and `sidewalk:*:bicycle` tags. Ways mapped separately, such as a `highway=cycleway`, are classified as themselves [ADR 0002].

Each way side gets one tier, or is excluded:

| Tier | Meaning |
|---|---|
| A | separated cycle track |
| B | shared foot and cycle path |
| C | physically buffered lane |
| D | painted lane, including advisory lanes |
| E | cycle street, and bus lanes open to cyclists |
| `excluded` | recorded for diagnostics, never counted in a preset |

The tiers derive from the tag logic of the ECF QECIO method, with documented differences. For example, a designated foot-and-cycle path with `segregated=yes` is tier B here, where QECIO counts it as a cycle track [ADR 0002].

Two lengths are stored for every way side. On the **`per_side`** basis each road side counts its full length, so a road with tracks on both sides counts twice. On the **`qecio_weighted`** basis one-way infrastructure counts half, as in QECIO [ADR 0002]. Lengths are computed in each city's projected coordinate system, never in degrees.

The rules are data, not code. A rule change is a new classifier version, and every run records the version it used [ADR 0005].

## Headline and presets

The headline is the length of tiers A and B, per side, per 100 km of main roads excluding motorways [ADR 0003]. Main roads are trunk, primary, secondary and tertiary roads with their links (`*_link`). Motorways are left out because cyclists cannot use them. A dual carriageway counts once.

"Protected" is reported as configurable sums of tiers, called presets. Each preset fixes the tiers it counts, the length basis and the road class it divides by. The presets reproduce the definitions of other publications on our own data [ADR 0002, ADR 0003]:

- `ours_headline` is the headline.
- `ecf_qecio` follows ECF QECIO v2.10. It counts tiers A to D on the QECIO basis and divides by main roads including motorways, with divided roads counted per carriageway, as QECIO does.
- `clean_cities_2025` follows the OSM fallback method of Clean Cities 2025. It divides tiers A and B by all public roads.

A preset whose basis is a parameter is computed and stored on both bases. The parameter only selects the reported one [ADR 0003; M2 R15]. Next to the ratios, absolute km and km per 100,000 residents are reported.

## Boundaries and population

Every city is computed on two boundaries [ADR 0004]:

- **LAU** is the city boundary: the Eurostat GISCO local administrative unit of 2024. It is the default. Where a city has no clean LAU polygon, the OSM administrative relation replaces it.
- **FUA** is the functional urban area, the city with its commuting zone. It is a robustness check.

Results are only compared within the same boundary kind. The LAU population comes from Eurostat. A city made of several LAU (`lau_codes`) has the sum of their figures, and only when every one has a figure. Where the LAU has no figure, the official population of the national statistics office, cited with year and URL in `config/cities.yaml` (`population_override`), supplies it. Without one, the identical NUTS3 unit supplies it [ADR 0004]. The code can also fall back to the Eurostat Urban Audit city population (`urb_cpop1`, set per city as `population_urau`), but no configured city uses it: the Urban Audit cities turned out to be greater cities, not the municipality [user decision of 2026-10-03, `docs/superpowers/rulings-m5.md`; `population` in `sources/boundaries.py`]. One population value applies to all snapshot years [M3 spec, decision 2]. FUA runs carry no population, so the FUA has no per-100k figure [M4 spec §4.4].

## Country trust gate

A city enters the report only when its country is trusted in `config/trust.yaml`. Cities of other countries can be run and diagnosed, but no report surface shows them [M5 spec §3.3]. Two checks come before a country is trusted:

- **Diagnostics.** They are computed from each city's current LAU run on the report classifier. They measure the share of cycling-tagged km that no rule recognises, the share that is excluded, tier B's share of A+B, and the area difference between the GISCO LAU polygon and the OSM relation. The thresholds are in `config/trust.yaml`. A value over its threshold needs a written note in the country's review file [M5 spec §3.1].
- **Golden sample.** About 12 ways per new country are chosen by query from the country's current snapshot. The tiers of each way are checked by hand and signed off. A test then holds the report classifier to them [M5 spec §3.2].

`velometer trust check` fails when

- a share threshold in `config/trust.yaml` is still the placeholder 1.0;
- a trusted country with cities in the configuration has no diagnostics file, its diagnostics leave out one of its cities, or they were computed on a run that is no longer the city's current LAU run;
- a diagnostic value breaks its threshold without a note in the country's review file;
- a trusted country was reviewed on a classifier other than the report classifier, or names none (`classifier_reviewed`);
- for a country with a golden sample: a golden way is unconfirmed, or confirmed ways no longer match the report classifier [M5 spec §3.3].

Germany needs no diagnostics file: it rests on the Berlin golden set of M1, which predates the trust gate. France, Poland, Austria and Denmark were trusted through the M2 reconciliation and have no golden sample of their own [M5 spec, decision 7].

## History snapshots

Yearly snapshots are taken at 1 January, 00:00 UTC, from Geofabrik full-history files. `osmium time-filter` cuts the state at that moment, and the result is clipped to the boundary [ADR 0005]. A history set is the group of region files that cover one boundary; its checksum identifies the files a series was built from [M3 R8]. Every city also has one current snapshot from public Geofabrik extracts; a FUA may need several merged extracts [M4 spec §3.2]. A current point follows a series only when it lies in the same year as the last snapshot.

## Flags

A rise in mapped km is not necessarily construction. Mapping effort is therefore diagnosed, not corrected [ADR 0006]. For every year, velometer counts the edits to cycling tags in the year before the snapshot, the number of mappers and the share of the most active mapper. No mapper id or name is stored. It also measures km on legacy tagging and km of road sides with cycling tags that no rule recognises.

Rules on these values flag a year for review. A flag changes no number. Change rules compare consecutive points only, never across a missing year. For the current point, the km minimums and the spike limit are scaled by the share of the year that has passed [M3 R14].

<!-- generated:flags -->
- `km_jump`: tier A+B km changes by at least 10 % of the previous value and by at least 25 km.
- `activity_spike`: the number of edits in the year reaches at least 2 times the median of the series years.
- `campaign_suspected`: `km_jump` and `activity_spike` in the same year.
- `single_mapper`: a `km_jump` year in which the most active mapper's share of the edits is at least 0.5.
- `tag_drift`: legacy km or unrecognised km changes by at least 50 % and by at least 5 km.

## Comparison with published figures

Our numbers are set against Clean Cities 2025, ECF QECIO v2.10, the BikeNetKit dataset and official city figures. Each external figure is stored with its source, its network scope, its length basis where known and its retrieval date [ADR 0001, ADR 0006, ADR 0008]. The comparison lists the gaps and explains them; it does not average them away. Clean Cities 2025 mostly used figures the cities submitted, and QECIO publishes results per NUTS3 region only. The rankings therefore differ in definition, denominator and boundary, not only in data.

## Provenance

Every number traces to a run. A run records the git commit, the classifier version and its parameters, a checksum of the classifier, preset and activity configuration, the OSM timestamp, the boundary checksum and the source files [ADR 0001; ADR 0005; M4 spec §3.1]. A report shows only runs of the report classifier and of the configuration on disk. Changing the configuration therefore needs new runs, and old runs are not mixed in.

## Known limitations

<!-- generated:limitations -->
- All OSM lengths are mapped km, not built km. A rise can come from construction, but also from a mapping campaign, an import or a change in tagging practice. No figure here states what a city has built [ADR 0006].
- The population is the LAU 2024 figure. Where the LAU has none, as for French communes, it is a cited national-statistics figure described below, or else the figure of the identical NUTS3 unit [ADR 0004] or of the Urban Audit city. The same value is used for every snapshot year, so per-100k values do not follow population change [M3 spec, decision 2].
- FUA runs carry no population, so the FUA has no per-100k figure [M4 spec §4.4; `city_boundary` in `sources/boundaries.py` returns a population only for the LAU]. The FUA serves as a robustness check [ADR 0004].
- The tiers do not consider width or surface. A narrow or rough cycle track counts like any other way of its tier. The share of lengths tagged with `width`, `surface` and `segregated` is recorded per tier as a diagnostic only [ADR 0002, ADR 0006].
- Tagging that the classifier recognises but does not treat as cycling infrastructure, for example `cycleway=shoulder` or an untyped `cycleway=yes`, is classified as excluded and counts in no preset (see the rules table). Road sides with cycling tags that no rule matches are reported as unrecognised km. They are not added to any tier [M3 R11; classifier v2, decided after M3].
- Clean Cities 2025 used city-submitted figures for most cities and OSM only as a fallback. The `clean_cities_2025` preset reproduces that fallback method, not the published numbers [ADR 0001, ADR 0003]. QECIO v2.10 publishes results only per NUTS3 region, frozen in January 2024. A NUTS3 region need not match the city boundary [ADR 0001].
- A run records the git commit it ran from. When the working tree had uncommitted changes, the commit carries the suffix `-dirty` and does not pin the code exactly. Most M3 history runs (classifier v1) carry it, because docs and scripts were uncommitted while they ran [M3 R19]. They are superseded by the M4 runs and not shown; no run in this report carries the suffix [M4 spec §3.4].
- History snapshots are cut from Geofabrik full-history files, which end on the date they were downloaded. The current run reads public extracts, whose OSM timestamp can be a different date. The mapping activity of the current point ends where the history file ends [M3 R9]. A current run merged from several regions takes the oldest region timestamp [M4 spec §3.2].
- The trust gate checks tagging practice, not whether the map is complete. Its thresholds catch cycling tags that the classifier does not recognise or excludes, an unusually high share of tier B in A+B, and an admin boundary that differs from the OSM relation [M5 spec §3.1]. The golden sample has about 12 ways per new country. That is too few to cover every local tagging habit [M5 spec §3.2]. France, Poland, Austria and Denmark have no golden sample of their own: they were trusted through the M2 reconciliation of their pilot cities [M5 spec, decision 7].
- Some cities have no LAU population, and neither their NUTS3 unit (a province or département) nor their Urban Audit city (a greater city) matches the municipality. Their population is the official figure of the national statistics office, cited with its year and URL in `config/cities.yaml` (`population_override`) [user decision of 2026-10-03, `docs/superpowers/rulings-m5.md`]. That year may not be 2024, so their per-100k values can rest on a different year than those of the other cities. The tables above give the year and source of each figure. This applies to Barcelona (INE Revisión del Padrón Municipal 2025 (1 Jan 2025)), Bordeaux (INSEE populations de référence 2023, population municipale), Lyon (INSEE populations de référence 2023, population municipale), Madrid (INE Revisión del Padrón Municipal 2025 (1 Jan 2025)) and Strasbourg (INSEE populations de référence 2023, population municipale).
- QECIO v2.10 publishes results per NUTS3 region. Where that region is not the city, the QECIO figure covers a different area than our runs. The city's QECIO comparison rows then carry the note "NUTS3 unit is not the city", and the rankings chart marks the city with † [M5 spec §4.3]. This applies to Amsterdam, Barcelona, Bordeaux, Bratislava, København, Helsinki, Ljubljana, Lyon, Madrid, Stockholm, Strasbourg and Utrecht.
